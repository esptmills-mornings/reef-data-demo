What unifying the data actually revealed

Five findings that only exist because the sources talk to each other.

Every number below is real — pulled from NOAA, AIMS, and the Queensland government's own primary data, not summarized from a report. The findings are ordered by how much cross-referencing each one needed.

01

The trigger for reef bleaching is quietly changing.

Cross-referencing satellite heat stress against the El Niño index across 26 years shows peak thermal stress at the Great Barrier Reef more than doubling since the 1990s — regardless of whether an El Niño is even present.

5.0 → 12.0peak DHW, 1998 → 2024
3 of 7recent bleaching years had no El Niño
02

Two reefs bleached the same year — for unrelated reasons, seven months apart.

2017 looked like one global event from either reef's dashboard alone. The unified timeline showed the Great Barrier Reef actually peaked in March and the Mesoamerican Reef in October — different hemispheres, different seasons, different causes: local wind conditions in the Pacific, a named Atlantic climate mode with no Pacific ENSO involvement at all.

7 monthsapart, not simultaneous
2 causesone basin each, unrelated
03

Raw tracking data corrected two cited storm categories on contact.

Published sources cited Cyclones Larry and Yasi at Category 5. The actual storm-track data, resolved to the specific reef entity, showed Category 4 at closest approach — the citation was each storm's lifetime peak intensity, recorded somewhere else entirely.

Cat 5 → 4corrected, both storms
308,468real storm-track rows checked
04

Coral and fish moved in opposite directions — because they answer to different forces.

At the Mesoamerican Reef, 2022 to 2024: live coral cover kept falling (climate-driven), while fish biomass rose sharply (fisheries-enforcement-driven). A single "reef health" score would have averaged the two together and hidden exactly the distinction a manager needs.

19% → 17%coral cover, falling
+40%fish biomass, rising
05

The same policy read as a 2-year success or a 7-year failure — depending only on scale.

Fish inside no-take marine reserves recovered within 2 years of the 2004 rezoning. The whole-fishery average — diluted by two-thirds of the reef staying open — didn't visibly turn around until 2011. Same policy, same data, two different answers depending purely on what grain you read it at.

2 yearszone-level recovery
7 yearsfishery-wide average catches up
